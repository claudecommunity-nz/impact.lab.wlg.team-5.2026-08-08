# Decisions

> Living doc — see [README.md](README.md). **Append, don't rewrite.** If a
> decision is reversed, add a new entry saying so and leave the old one; the
> reasoning that turned out wrong is the useful part.

Format: what was decided, why, and what it costs.

---

### 1. Two schemas, not one table with a `is_public` flag

*8 August 2026*

`silver` holds full fidelity, `gold` is a filtered projection, and `silver` is
absent from `config.toml`'s exposed schemas so PostgREST cannot serve it at all.

**Why:** a flag has to be checked correctly every time. A schema that is not
exposed has no URL. Containment that depends on no policy being wrong is worth
more than containment that depends on every policy being right.

**Costs:** every new field has to be added twice — once in silver, once in the
gold view. That friction is deliberate: adding a column to the public projection
should be a decision, not a side effect.

---

### 2. The status lifecycle has eight states, the app knows five

*8 August 2026*

`gold.report` publishes both `status` (eight) and `legacyStatus` (mapped to the
app's five).

**Why:** "an agency has it" and "the agency says it is done" are different facts
and a resident deserves to be told which applies. Forcing the app to change
first would have blocked the schema.

**Cost:** two vocabularies in the API. The mapping is lossy in the safe
direction — `fixed` maps to `acting`, not `resolved`, because a resident reading
"Resolved" about a slip still blocking their street is exactly the failure this
project exists to prevent.

---

### 3. Location coarsening is data, not code

*8 August 2026*

`fault_type.default_precision`, overridable per report.

**Why:** the judgement is editorial and will change. A blocked road is useful at
street level and identifies nobody; "six houses up the lane have no vehicle
access" identifies a household. Changing that call should be an `update`, not a
deploy.

**Also:** the rules are published on `gold.fault_type`, so a resident can check
what happens to their pin before dropping it.

---

### 4. Fuzz in NZTM2000, snap to cell centre

*8 August 2026*

**Why:** EPSG:2193 is metric, so a 100m cell is exactly 100m, and it is the
projection every WCC source dataset is already published in. Centre rather than
corner so the pin sits where the zone actually is.

**Rejected:** H3 hexagons — the extension is not available on Supabase. A square
grid is honest and exact.

---

### 5. Unverified reports are shown, labelled — not hidden

*8 August 2026*

Every gold row carries `verificationLevel`, `isSynthetic` and a `disclaimer`
worded for its actual state.

**Why:** hiding unverified reports loses the signal WCC wants. Showing them
without the label is the failure mode the problem statements are most wary of.
Putting the disclaimer on the row means a consumer who never read the docs still
cannot present it as fact.

---

### 6. Clustering promotes to `corroborated`, and stops there

*8 August 2026*

3+ reports of the same fault type within 250m promote `unverified` members to
`corroborated`. Never higher, never downgrading a human judgement.

**Why:** it is the only automatic trust change in the system, so it should be
the most conservative one available. Corroborated is not confirmed, and the
disclaimer says so.

**Cost:** clusters are a proximity heuristic and can group two genuinely
different incidents. `radiusM` and `groupedBy` are published so the claim is
checkable rather than magic.

---

### 7. Life-safety categories are refused at the database

*8 August 2026*

`fault_type.intake_blocked`; `gold.submit_report()` raises rather than writing.

**Why:** the form already hides them, but a prototype that quietly absorbs a
life-safety report because someone posted straight to the API would be worse
than no prototype. The database does not trust the form to have done it.

---

### 8. Column names mirror the existing Council payload

*8 August 2026*

`faultType`, `faultDesc`, `loc*`, `contact*`, `externalSystemName`, `sourceType`.

**Why:** a report from this channel could be handed to the same downstream
Council queue with no translation layer. That is the argument for this being a
real channel rather than a parallel one — which is the actual problem statement.

---

### 9. `advance_status` is not granted to `anon`

*8 August 2026*

**Why:** moving a report to "Completed & confirmed" is a Council statement about
the world. A public key must not be able to make it, or the acknowledgement
trail stops meaning anything. The console's PATCH route runs server-side with
the service role key.

---

### 10. Reference data lives in the seed generator, not in migrations

*8 August 2026*

`supabase/seed.sql` is generated by `scripts/build-seed.mjs` and truncates
`agency`, `service` and `fault_type` before reloading them.

**Why:** discovered the hard way — the seed runs *after* migrations on
`db reset`, so reference rows inserted by a migration are silently wiped.
Migrations own structure; the generator owns rows.

---

### 10a. Reversed — reference data lives in migrations after all

*8 August 2026, later the same morning*

Reverses **10**. `scripts/build-seed.mjs` was changed so `seed.sql` truncates
only reports and hubs, and `agency`, `service` and `fault_type` are owned by
`20260808000009_reference_seed.sql`.

**Why the reversal is right:** WCC's ownership and priority classification is
not demo data. Putting it in the seed meant a `db reset` could silently discard
a Council judgement; putting it in a migration versions it like the schema it
constrains.

**What it cost, immediately:** `intake_blocked` was set by the old seed and the
migration does not carry the column, so life-safety intake blocking silently
turned off — verified by successfully submitting an `assistance` report as
`anon`. Moving data between owners drops whatever the new owner forgets to
carry. See [workflow-gaps.md](workflow-gaps.md).

**Decision 10 stays above** because its reasoning — that the seed truncates
after migrations run — is still true and still a trap. The fix was to stop the
seed truncating reference tables, not to move the data.

---

### 11. Priority is called priority, and never auto-escalates to 1

*8 August 2026* — applied, see [classification.md](classification.md)

WCC's "job status" 1–4 is stored as `priority`, published with a
`priorityBasis`, and a reporter marking something urgent lifts the category
default by at most one step, never to 1.

**Why:** `status` already means lifecycle here. And a reporter saying "urgent" is
evidence, not an assessment — level 1 commits WCC to an immediate cordon, which
a human has to decide.

---

### 12. Merged categories retire as aliases, never as deletions

*8 August 2026* — `20260808000015`

Three pairs were merged: `flooding`+`coastal` → `surface-flood`,
`road-blocked`+`access-cut` → `road-closure`, `power-out`+`water-out` →
`service-outage`. The six old codes keep their rows, go `is_active = false`, and
point at the new one through `superseded_by`. `gold.submit_report` resolves the
pointer and tells the caller via `faultTypeRemapped`.

**Why:** someone standing in front of water coming over a sea wall onto a road
should not have to decide whether that is "flooding" or "coastal inundation"
before they can tell anyone. Fewer, clearer boxes mean more reports and fewer
miscategorised ones.

**Why aliases rather than deletion:** deleting the codes would have broken every
client built against the old list, on the one day nobody has time to fix them —
including our own form, which still sends them. A merge that breaks integrations
is a merge nobody dares run.

Three things the merge would have quietly lost, each handled rather than
absorbed: the stricter location precision is inherited, not the looser one;
`service-outage` routes to no agency rather than confidently sending every burst
main to the power company; and `surface-flood` keeps `wcc_lead` for the common
case while carrying the WCC/GWRC coastal split in its `ownership_note`.

---

### 13. Scope drift is published, not enforced

*8 August 2026* — `20260808000016`

WCC named six categories it leads and four it shares. That is recorded as data in
`silver.wcc_scope` and compared against the live classification by
`gold.scope_audit`, which is readable by `anon`. It is **not** a constraint.

**Why not a constraint:** during a build the classification is still moving, and
a constraint that blocks a legitimate edit gets dropped rather than obeyed —
which loses the check entirely. A view that says "this category claims WCC lead
but is not in WCC's stated scope" survives being ignored.

**Why it matters:** a seventh category appearing later with `wcc_lead` on it
would put Council's name against a job Council never accepted, and nothing would
have noticed. The audit is restricted to `service = 'emergency'` for the same
reason — auditing potholes and graffiti produces thirty rows of noise that trains
everyone to ignore the four that matter.

This is the same principle as `priorityBasis` and `verificationLevel`: where the
system is uncertain or inferring, it says so rather than presenting a clean
answer.
